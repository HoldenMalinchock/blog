---
title: AI Inevitability
publish_date: 2026-06-06
---

The AI revolution is here and it's here to stay — that part feels inevitable. But the more time we spend inside this new reality, the clearer it becomes that the worker apocalypse everyone braced for isn't arriving on schedule. The reason is boring and rarely discussed: AI is too expensive. Today, the person it replaces is often cheaper than the AI that replaced them.

Promises of AI replacing non technical admin work was once too good to be true, however models are accelerating at paces never before seen. This reality looks more possible with every model that's released. But in the wild, the state of play may not be as easy.

Companies across the market adopted AI at large, it's a copycat world. Encouraging more employees to use AI to increase efficiency and accuracy of mundane tasks. The numbers are pretty clear it's done just that. Workers are more efficient than ever, and if you've ever used it you know. But all this efficiency comes at a cost that seems to be a problem across all major companies.

AI use is too expensive. Uber blew through their rumored $4.3B 2026 AI budget in just 4 months. Countless other companies are reporting the same thing, backpedaling the great revolution as companies start spending more on AI than they do on employees. I think this is one of the major unsolved problems with AI. For every employee you replace with AI, at least today, that employee is replaced with someone else who uses AI to do the job of two people. But the cost of AI to replace that person is starting to cost more than it would to fill the role with a human. And in some cases it's not just a small amount more.

This is a huge problem for AI to solve, and local/free AI models are not close to the complex models. While workers can't be fully replaced even with the top models, though it marches closer by the day, the free/local models are even further. Compounding this problem and perhaps the most concerning is all the model providers, the OpenAI's, Anthropics, xAI's of the world are losing money hand over fist. Companies are unable to support AI costs and that's with AI companies operating at huge losses. This is not sustainable on either end.

The only way for these companies to reach profitability is to increase prices which is already perceived as too high. An inevitable collision course of rising prices and unprofitable companies propped up on promises of profit later. It's a standoff with two sides pulling in opposite directions: companies using AI need to cut their costs without cutting their usage, and AI companies need to charge more just to survive. Something has to give.

Ironically as the old saying goes; Don't dig for gold, sell shovels. And nobody is doing this better than Nvidia, the only company who is making any money off AI right now. It feels we are already on the brink of critical mass where people are cheaper than AI and this only became possible to replace some roles in the last 1-2 years.

Websites like [isaiprofitable.com](https://isaiprofitable.com) show the real costs and losses these companies are facing and it's staggering. Acquisitions feel inevitable at this point where companies like Apple, Amazon, Meta buy up AI companies because they can consume the huge losses while they wait for profitability.

If the cost of AI doesn't go down, which seems untenable, entry level workers are going to be just fine. And I'd advise any college age software engineer to get on board or get off the ship. Competing with AI is a losing proposition as your value over AI comes with experience. Your real value is stepping into roles functioning as 2 employees with your AI proficiency. Provide more value for your price than the person next to you. Never more than now has your career been analogous to selling a product against other products. "Sell yourself" has always been true but now more than ever it's about giving a company a reason to pay you rather than having a senior engineer run local models to do your job.
